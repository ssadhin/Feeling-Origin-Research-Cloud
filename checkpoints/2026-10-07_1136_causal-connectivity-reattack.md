BUILD
A new 2026 human intracranial preprint (Lyu et al., PMID 42620125; 63 individuals, 660 cingulate/precuneus stimulation sites) is unusually relevant to the mechanism class. Neighboring sites separated by millimeters produced categorically different subjective bodily experiences or remained silent; differences were associated with causal connectivity profiles rather than anatomical location alone. Connectivity between cingulate/precuneus and posterior insula was reported as a critical determinant of whether local stimulation produced a reportable bodily experience. This supports replacing a single-region M with a connectivity/embedding mechanism class.

ATTACK
This does not identify affective feeling. Outcomes are bodily/sensorimotor/vestibular/complex subjective reports, not independently measured valence/unpleasantness. Reportability is part of the endpoint, so access/report cannot be separated from phenomenal presence. No matched reasoning task is preserved. The paper is a preprint, not independent replication. Thus it cannot satisfy the proof target and cannot establish phenomenality from stimulation reports alone.

RE-ATTACK / TRIANGULATION
Lockwood et al. 2013 TMS over S2 causally disrupted pain-intensity discrimination while sparing nociceptive localization, strengthening sensory-component separability but not affect. Guillory & Bujarski 2019 iES showed stimulation magnitude in OFC/ACC/insula covaried with subjective/emotional intensity, but stimulation commonly co-produced sensory/visceral experiences, defeating affect specificity. A 2026 ketamine placebo-controlled study (Goldway et al., Neuropsychopharmacology, published 20 July 2026; n=37) found analgesia and dissociation with distinct neural/behavioral correlates, but it does not preserve sensory discrimination while selectively changing unpleasantness and therefore is not the required dissociation.

REFINEMENT
Candidate M should now be formalized as affect-specific causal embedding: a connectivity-defined transformation by which an evaluated content becomes causally integrated into an affective significance circuit. This remains a hypothesis class, not a discovered mechanism. Anatomical locus alone is explicitly rejected.

FROZEN KILL CONDITIONS
Kill this M if (1) connectivity/embedding predicts report/access but not affect after matched sensory intensity and autonomic/arousal controls; (2) a feed-forward valuation/salience model predicts held-out affect equally well; (3) perturbing the candidate pathway fails to alter valence/unpleasantness within preregistered sensitivity while sensory discrimination and reasoning are preserved; (4) reasoning/access perturbation abolishes affect whenever reasoning/access falls; or (5) independent replication fails.

NOVELTY / PUBLICATION AUDIT
Connectivity-dependent subjective effects are prior art and the 2026 study itself directly claims them. The potentially novel contribution would require an affect-specific, preregistered double dissociation plus held-out rival-model victory. No proof-grade or paper-worthy result emerged in this run.

Prioritize human causal datasets/studies with pathway-targeted perturbation (SEEG/iES/LIFU/TMS) AND separate unpleasantness/valence from sensory discrimination/intensity. Freeze equivalence margins before inspecting outcomes. Treat Lyu 2026 as mechanistic prior, not confirmatory evidence.
