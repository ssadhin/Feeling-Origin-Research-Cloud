# Checkpoint 2026-10-05

Feeling Origin reactivated by explicit user instruction. Rejected F=(G*C*S*O)^(1/4); ownership not necessary. Keep phenomenal presence P separate from affective significance S.

Evidence: Frisch et al. 2024 N=72: cues linked to forgotten negative images increased pain unpleasantness (5.52 vs 5.23, p=.02), while intensity was unchanged (5.27 vs 5.16, p=.30). This supports history-dependent affect modulation without explicit recall, but does not prove phenomenality or unique mechanism. Zidda et al. 2024 similarly found negative context increased unpleasantness but not intensity. Strohman et al. 2024 dACC LIFU N=16 reduced pain ratings by 1.09 +/- 0.20 vs sham, t(15)=-5.39, p=7.46e-5 and P2 CHEP by 38.1%, but did not separate intensity/unpleasantness. In et al. 2024 PI LIFU N=16 attenuated temporal summation ratings but does not isolate affect.

Verdict: no proof-grade M. Main rivals remain sensory processing, salience/attention, autonomic regulation, unconscious valuation, predictive appraisal, and access/report effects. Established sensory-affective pain dissociation is prior art and not paper-worthy alone.

Next: causal evidence matrix across human interventions with separate unpleasantness, intensity/discrimination, autonomic, access/report, reasoning, blinding, effect size, replication. Freeze held-out rival-model prediction before extraction.

Kill candidate M if selective affect effect vanishes under matched sensory/autonomic/valuation variables, if general access/reasoning is equivalently impaired, if simpler rival predicts held-out interventions equally well, or independent replication fails.
