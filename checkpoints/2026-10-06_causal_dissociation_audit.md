# Feeling Origin checkpoint — 2026-10-06

Research resumed. Preserve P (phenomenal presence) distinct from S (affective significance); ownership is not assumed necessary; earlier geometric-mean formula remains rejected.

Frozen criterion: candidate M must selectively alter unpleasantness/valence with sensory discrimination and reasoning approximately preserved, plus converse reasoning/access disruption with affect preserved. Behavior/report alone cannot establish phenomenality.

Evidence: PMID 31053232 systematic review found no compelling selective cognitive modulation across 12 qualifying studies. PMID 39245849 reported negative primes increased unpleasantness but not intensity. PMID 38666650 found forgotten negative cues increased unpleasantness (5.52 vs 5.23, p=.02) without significant intensity change (5.27 vs 5.16, p=.30). PMID 38182418 dACC focused ultrasound reduced pain ratings and altered autonomic measures, so not cleanly affect-specific. PMID 39089647 posterior-insula focused ultrasound reduced temporal summation but did not isolate unpleasantness. PMID 25577137 preserved/excessive pain affect after extensive bilateral insula/ACC/amygdala damage is a counterexample to simple regional necessity.

Verdict: no proof-grade M. Current survivor is a distributed, history-sensitive affective-significance process. Double causal dissociation, out-of-sample prediction, rival comparison and independent replication are not jointly met.

Novelty: known sensory/unpleasantness dissociation is prior art. Potential contribution is a preregistered cross-dataset causal-dissociation framework separating P, S, unconscious valuation, autonomic response, report, access/metacognition and reasoning.

Kill conditions: specificity disappears after sensory/discrimination/demand matching; arousal/salience explains equally well; perturbation acts through sensory evidence; reasoning/access disruption abolishes affect; or out-of-sample generalization fails.
