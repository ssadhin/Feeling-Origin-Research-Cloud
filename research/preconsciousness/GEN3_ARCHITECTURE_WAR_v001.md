G3-A — TYPED LATTICE / MULTISYSTEM MODEL
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS META-ONTOLOGY, NOT AS A SINGLE MECHANISM THEORY.
Strength:
Keeps regulation, action disposition, motivation, emotion episode, phenomenality and report at different ontological levels.
Accommodates recurrent interaction and phenomenon-specific paths.
Explains why no four-node serial order is universal.
Weakness:
So flexible that it can become descriptive rather than predictive unless individual edges/mechanisms are frozen and tested.
Does not solve the mechanism of phenomenality.
Residual coverage:
R1–R7: strong organizational coverage.
R8: explicitly unresolved.
R9: handles by making universality an empirical question.
R10: strong because it distinguishes mechanisms from summaries/measurements.
==================================================
G3-B — COMPONENT-ONLY EMOTION MODEL
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS / STRONG MINIMAL RIVAL.
Claim:
Emotion is not an extra causal substance/node. It is the temporally coordinated trajectory of appraisal, physiology, motivation/action tendency, expression, attention/learning and (depending on definition) feeling.
Support:
Component-process and recent componential syntheses explicitly describe interacting emotion components and synchronization.
Network/connectivity evidence argues against overly simple hierarchical/top-down emotion processing.
Strength:
Avoids inventing a hidden central state when observed/intervenable components suffice.
Weakness:
May miss a genuine low-dimensional latent state if one causally coordinates components.
Kill condition:
If a manipulable latent variable predicts and causally controls multiple components beyond the component network itself, component-only is incomplete.
==================================================
G3-C — LATENT-STATE EMOTION MODEL
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS EMPIRICAL IMPLEMENTATION HYPOTHESIS, NOT DEFINITION.
Claim:
A latent state e_t causally coordinates many outputs.
Strength:
Compact; matches functional-state criteria such as persistence, scalability and generalization.
Weakness:
Latent-variable fit alone can merely summarize correlated outputs.
Without interventions or invariance tests, the model does not prove an extra central causal entity.
Kill condition:
No independent predictive/causal increment after observed component dynamics, arousal, motivation, body state and context are included.
RELATION TO G3-B
G3-B and G3-C may be observationally equivalent in some datasets.
The distinction is causal/mechanistic, not lexical.
==================================================
G3-D — CONSTRUCTIONIST / CORE-AFFECT-FIRST
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS SERIOUS HUMAN-EMOTION RIVAL; NOT UNIVERSAL META-ONTOLOGY.
Claim:
Core affect plus conceptualization/context constructs emotion instances/categories.
Strength:
Explains category heterogeneity, context sensitivity and conceptual influence.
Separates core affect from full emotional episodes.
Weakness:
Does not by itself settle cross-species primary-process claims and may require different treatment of nonconceptual affective organization.
Residuals:
Strong for R6 and category heterogeneity.
Does not solve R8 phenomenality.
==================================================
G3-E — APPRAISAL / COMPONENT-PROCESS
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS STRONG PROCESS THEORY.
Claim:
Multi-level appraisal of relevance/concerns organizes component changes, with feeling as an integrated component in standard CPM formulations.
Strength:
Explicit temporal/component architecture and many measurable variables.
Weakness:
Making appraisal the dominant elicitor may be too theory-specific for a universal definition.
Because standard CPM includes feeling as a component of emotion, it cannot independently establish E-versus-F using terminology alone.
Residuals:
Strong for emotion episode dynamics and R5/R6.
Does not explain why every feeling family should be an emotion; therefore R1/R3 force broader F taxonomy.
==================================================
G3-F — PRIMARY-PROCESS / EVOLUTIONARY AFFECT
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS A DISTINCT EVOLUTIONARY THEORY FAMILY.
Claim:
Ancient subcortical affective/emotional systems organize primary-process emotions/feelings.
Strength:
Cross-species causal perturbation tradition using stimulation, pharmacology and lesions.
Explicitly distinguishes homeostatic affects such as hunger/thirst from primary emotional affects in Pankseppian work.
Weakness:
Terminology makes affective feeling central and does not map cleanly to a nonphenomenal functional-state E definition.
Does not by itself explain complex conceptual/social emotion diversity.
Residuals:
R1 is accommodated by separate homeostatic affects.
R5 remains theory-dependent.
==================================================
G3-G — HOMEOSTATIC-FEELING-FIRST
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS FOR A SUBDOMAIN; FAIL AS UNIVERSAL ARCHITECTURE.
Claim:
Homeostatic/interoceptive regulation can give rise directly to felt hunger, thirst, pain, wellbeing, etc. in feeling-capable organisms.
Strength:
Strong prior art and directly attacks universal E -> F mediation.
Explains R1.
Weakness:
Cannot explain metacognitive feelings, all emotional feelings or all sensory experiences by homeostatic content alone.
Generation consequence:
F must be content-indexed and multi-route.
==================================================
G3-H — PREDICTIVE / ACTIVE-INFERENCE UNIFIED MODEL
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS A POWERFUL UNIFYING FORMALISM; BLOCKED AS UNIQUE ONTOLOGY.
Claim:
Hierarchical prediction/control over interoceptive, proprioceptive and exteroceptive variables can unify homeostatic/allostatic regulation, action, learning, affect and emotion.
Strength:
Provides one mathematical language across traditionally separated mechanisms.
Weakness:
A shared formalism does not prove the constructs are one thing.
Empirical evidence for specific interoceptive-inference mechanisms varies, and phenomenality is not entailed by free-energy minimization/prediction alone.
Residuals:
Potentially addresses R1–R7.
R8 remains unresolved.
R10 remains crucial: one formalism can model different ontological roles.
==================================================
G3-I — ACTION/MOTIVATION-FIRST
==================================================
VERDICT: PARTIAL.
Claim:
Emotion is fundamentally action readiness/motivational organization.
Strength:
Captures organism-environment function and links appraisal to adaptive action.
Weakness:
RDoC explicitly separates motivation, arousal, valence and motor action; metacognitive/homeostatic feelings and mood cannot all be reduced to one action-tendency variable.
Useful component, not universal ontology.
==================================================
G3-J — PHENOMENAL-FAMILY MODEL
==================================================
VERDICT: PASS AS THE STRONGEST CURRENT FEELING ONTOLOGY.
Claim:
"Feeling" is a family of content-specific phenomenal states rather than one upstream generator.
Strength:
Handles homeostatic, emotional, sensory-affective, metacognitive/epistemic, agentive and other feeling families.
Preserves distinction between phenomenal occurrence and access/report.
Weakness:
Provides taxonomy/measurement logic, not the common physical mechanism of phenomenality.
A future consciousness theory might show a shared mechanism across all families, but current evidence does not justify assuming one.
==================================================
CROSS-ARCHITECTURE COMPARISON
==================================================
1. G3-B vs G3-C
Not decided by definitions.
Component-only and latent-state implementations can fit the same observations.
Requires causal intervention/model-invariance tests.
2. G3-D vs G3-E vs G3-F vs G3-H vs G3-I
These are not all mutually exclusive:
- constructionist theory emphasizes conceptualization/core affect;
- appraisal theory emphasizes relevance evaluation;
- primary-process theory emphasizes evolutionarily ancient affective systems;
- active inference supplies a computational formalism;
- motivational theory emphasizes action tendency.
Recent componential synthesis suggests these theory families often emphasize different components of a larger emotion system.
3. G3-G vs any universal E->F theory
G3-G wins as a counterexample class: homeostatic feeling need not be classified as a full emotion episode.
Therefore universal E->F is rejected.
4. G3-J vs one-node F
G3-J wins because metacognitive, homeostatic and emotional feelings have distinct proximal content/generators and no established single mechanism below phenomenality.
5. G3-A vs all single-theory models
G3-A survives as the organizing meta-model because it can state which ontological role each theory addresses without claiming all theory families are equivalent.

GENERATION-4 OPTIONS GENERATED FROM THE WAR
==================================================
G4-1 — THREE-LEVEL FRAMEWORK
Separate:
LEVEL O — ONTOLOGY: what type of entity is under discussion?
process, disposition, episode/trajectory, representation, phenomenal state, measurement.
LEVEL M — MECHANISM: what generates/implements it?
homeostatic/allostatic regulation, developmental canalization, reflex/motor circuitry, appraisal, predictive inference, component coupling, etc.
LEVEL X — MEASUREMENT/ACCESS: how is it observed?
physiology, behavior, reports, ratings, metacognition, neural measures.
This prevents theories at different explanatory levels from being falsely treated as direct competitors.
G4-2 — TYPED PROCESS LATTICE
Core typed set:
V = viability constraints.
M_C = constitutive self-maintenance.
M_R = regulatory self-maintenance.
D = drive/motivation.
P = prepared action architecture, itself decomposable into reflex/policy/generator types.
A = arousal.
C = appraisal/valuation/core-affect-related variables.
E = emotion coordination episode/trajectory.
F^(c) = content-indexed phenomenal feeling family.
X = access/report.
The causal graph is recurrent and phenomenon-specific, not one total order.
G4-3 — FOUR-TARGET PROJECTION
If the original four words must be preserved for communication:
SELF-MAINTENANCE = umbrella over M_C/M_R with V as criterion.
INSTINCT = historical umbrella over prepared action architectures; never a scalar.
EMOTION = label for a coordinated episode/trajectory; mechanism theory remains open.
FEELING = family of first-person affective/interoceptive/metacognitive phenomenal states; report separate.
This is a projection of G4-2, not the full ontology.
G4-4 — MODEL-SELECTION PROGRAM FOR E
Keep both:
E_component and E_latent.
Do not choose until causal data distinguish them.
G4-5 — PHENOMENAL-BRIDGE PROGRAM FOR F
Keep feeling-family taxonomy separate from the unknown common/heterogeneous mechanism Phi that makes any content phenomenal.
