A. SELF-MAINTENANCE
==================================================
M1 FIXED-SETPOINT HOMEOSTASIS — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Strong for some regulated variables but too narrow as a general definition. Contemporary homeostasis uses multiple loops, and allostatic/predictive regulation can change defended values or act before error appears.
M2 DYNAMIC/VARIABLE-SETPOINT HOMEOSTASIS — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Improves on M1 and captures context-dependent reference ranges, but still focuses on regulated variables rather than constitutive repair/self-production.
M3 ALLOSTATIC/PREDICTIVE REGULATION — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Well-supported anticipatory regulation. It is a regulatory strategy within self-maintenance, not the entirety of self-maintenance.
M4 VIABILITY-REGION/VIABILITY-KERNEL CONTROL — PASS-FORMALISM / PASS-CORE.
Strong mathematical representation of keeping states inside viability constraints. It formalizes a central consequence of self-maintenance but does not by itself specify constitutive self-production or repair.
M5 ORGANIZATIONAL CLOSURE/AUTOPOIETIC SELF-PRODUCTION — PASS-CORE, THEORY-LADEN.
Captures production/maintenance of the organization that makes the organism a persisting living unity. Strong alternative to pure setpoint accounts. More abstract and not identical to physiological homeostasis.
M6 METABOLIC/REPAIR MAINTENANCE — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Necessary in many organisms, but too narrow as the whole definition because regulation includes more than repair/turnover.
M7 ACTIVE-INFERENCE/SELF-EVIDENCING — PASS-FORMALISM, NOT DEFINITION.
Useful predictive-control framework. It is one candidate computational formalism for regulation, not evidence that all self-maintenance is literally active inference.
M8 PERSISTENCE/SURVIVAL-PROBABILITY OBJECTIVE — FAIL AS DEFINITION; PASS AS OUTCOME METRIC.
Survival/persistence is an outcome/goal-level quantity. It does not identify the mechanism and can conflate self-maintenance with external protection, reproduction, or luck.
M9 MULTISCALE VIABILITY REGULATION — PASS-CORE.
Generation-0 umbrella survives, but comparison exposes a missing constitutive dimension: viable state preservation and self-production/constraint regeneration need not be identical.
M-RESULT
No single candidate completely dominates. The strongest combination is M4 + M5 + M9 with M1/M2/M3/M6 as mechanisms/subprocesses and M7 as an optional formalism. This generates M10.

B. INSTINCT / PREPARED ACTION
==================================================
I1 GENETICALLY HARDWIRED PROGRAM — FAIL.
Genetic influence does not imply a complete pre-specified behavioral program; development and species-typical experience matter.
I2 PRESENT-AT-BIRTH — FAIL AS DEFINITION.
Many species-typical behaviors mature later; being present at birth is neither necessary nor sufficient for the broad target.
I3 EXPERIENCE-INDEPENDENT — FAIL.
Developmental-system evidence directly rejects the idea that species-typical behavior generally develops without experience.
I4 SPECIES-TYPICAL — PASS-SUBTYPE/PROPERTY, NOT SUFFICIENT.
Population regularity is relevant but can also characterize learned behaviors.
I5 FIXED/MODAL ACTION PATTERN — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Valid ethological action-pattern concept in suitable systems, but too narrow for all instinct-like behavior.
I6 REFLEX — NEIGHBOR.
A reflex can be innate, but reflex architecture is not equivalent to the full class of prepared/instinctive behavior.
I7 RDoC INNATE MOTOR PATTERN — PASS-CORE FOR MOTOR DOMAIN.
Officially defined as unlearned action plans triggered by internal/external stimuli. Useful but deliberately motor-focused.
I8 DEVELOPMENTALLY CANALIZED ACTION DISPOSITION (ICAD) — PASS-CORE.
Survives the developmental critique because it allows normal developmental input and focuses on reliable action bias rather than genetic preprogramming.
I9 PRIOR OVER POLICIES/PREPAREDNESS — PASS-CORE / FORMALIZATION.
Strong computational rendering of pre-task action bias. Needs developmental constraints so that an arbitrary learned prior is not mislabeled instinctive.
I10 DEVELOPMENTAL ATTRACTOR/CANALIZATION — PASS-CORE / FORMALISM.
Captures reliable developmental convergence but does not alone specify that the outcome is an action-policy disposition.
I11 DRIVE/MOTIVATION — NEIGHBOR.
Internal state that modulates direction, vigor, persistence, and action selection; action-selection literature explicitly distinguishes such states from the selection mechanism.
I12 ACTION TENDENCY — NEIGHBOR / EMOTION COMPONENT.
A readiness for action can occur within emotion theory and should not be equated with instinct.
I13 LEARNED HABIT — NEIGHBOR / CONTRAST CLASS.
RDoC defines habit as learned stimulus-response mapping; useful negative control against prepared action.
I14 CENTRAL PATTERN GENERATOR/MOTOR PROGRAM — PASS-SUBTYPE/MECHANISM.
Can instantiate organized motor output and sometimes innate motor infrastructure, but a CPG is implementation machinery, not the whole dispositional category.
I15 ACTION-SELECTION ARCHITECTURE — NEIGHBOR/SUPERSET.
Includes innate and learned policies; too broad to define instinct specifically.
I-RESULT
Broad instinct is confirmed as a family/clutter term. The strongest scientific replacement is the intersection of I8 + I9 + I10: a developmentally canalized prepared policy landscape, with I7/I5/I14 as motor subtypes/implementations. This generates I16.

C. EMOTION
==================================================
E1 DISCRETE BASIC-EMOTION PROGRAM — PASS AS THEORY FAMILY/SUBTYPE; FAIL AS UNIVERSAL DEFINITION.
Substantial evidence exists for evolved response systems, but the field does not converge on a fixed small set of universal natural kinds.
E2 PRIMARY-PROCESS AFFECTIVE SYSTEM — PASS AS THEORY FAMILY.
Pankseppian systems are serious mechanistic candidates, but their taxonomy includes affective feeling commitments and distinguishes homeostatic affects from emotional affects.
E3 FUNCTIONAL CENTRAL EMOTION-STATE — PASS-CORE WITH REVISION.
Adolphs-style functional-state definition is coherent and supports E/F separation. The word "central" risks overcommitting to one latent node rather than a distributed coordination pattern.
E4 COMPONENT-PROCESS/SYNCHRONIZATION EPISODE — PASS-CORE.
Strong integrative candidate: emotion as dynamic coordination among appraisal, physiology, action tendency, expression, feeling and cognitive effects.
E5 APPRAISAL STATE/PROCESS — PASS-SUBTYPE/ELICITATION COMPONENT.
Strong component and theory family, but appraisal alone is not the whole emotion episode.
E6 CORE AFFECT — NEIGHBOR / CONSTRUCTIONIST INGREDIENT.
Consciously accessible pleasure/activation can exist as mood/background and does not equal a full prototypical emotion episode.
E7 PSYCHOLOGICALLY CONSTRUCTED EMOTION — PASS AS THEORY FAMILY.
Important rival: emotion categories may be constructed from more basic domain-general processes. Prevents assuming fixed biological emotion kinds.
E8 ACTION-TENDENCY/MOTIVATIONAL STATE — PASS-SUBTYPE/COMPONENT.
Action tendency is one major emotion component, not sufficient for whole emotion.
E9 INTEROCEPTIVE/BODY-STATE REPRESENTATION — PASS-SUBTYPE/THEORY FAMILY.
Important bodily/interoceptive contribution but insufficient as universal whole-emotion definition.
E10 PREDICTIVE/ALLOSTATIC EMOTION STATE — PASS AS THEORY FAMILY.
Plausible computational architecture, especially in constructionist/predictive accounts; not uniquely established.
E11 DISTRIBUTED DYNAMICAL COORDINATION PATTERN — PASS-CORE.
Avoids assuming one central latent node and fits component-interaction evidence.
E12 SOCIAL-RELATIONAL EPISODE — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Critical for many emotions but not all.
E13 EMOTION CONCEPT/CATEGORY/LABEL — NEIGHBOR.
Adolphs explicitly separates concepts of emotion from emotion states. Constructionist theories give conceptualization a stronger role, but the label/category is not theory-neutrally identical to emotion.
E14 EXPRESSION/RESPONSE PATTERN — PASS-SUBTYPE/MEASUREMENT.
Expression is one component/output; not sufficient as the whole emotion.
E15 UMBRELLA/FAMILY TERM — PASS AT FIELD-LEVEL.
The field uses "emotion" for several related targets. A theory-neutral research program must distinguish functional state, full componential episode, category/concept, expression, and conscious experience.
E-RESULT
Generation-0 E survives only after a major refinement: do not require a single central latent state. The strongest cross-theory option is a two-level distinction:
E_func = third-person functional coordination organization excluding phenomenality by definition;
E_episode = full componential emotion episode, whose theory-specific definition may include feeling.
This generates E16.

D. FEELING
==================================================
F1 ALL PHENOMENAL CONSCIOUSNESS — FAIL AS ORDINARY AFFECTIVE DEFINITION; PASS AS MAXIMAL PHILOSOPHICAL USAGE.
Too broad for affective science because visual/auditory phenomenology would count automatically.
F2 AFFECTIVE PHENOMENAL EXPERIENCE — PASS-CORE FOR AFFECTIVE FEELING.
Good generic target, but may omit sensory and metacognitive uses of "feeling."
F3 MENTAL EXPERIENCE OF BODY STATE — PASS-CORE AS DAMASIO-STYLE FAMILY.
Strong for homeostatic and bodily feeling; too narrow for every usage of feeling unless higher cognitive/social feelings are grounded through body-state representations.
F4 SUBJECTIVE EMOTIONAL EXPERIENCE — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Clear and common use, but excludes homeostatic, sensory and metacognitive feelings.
F5 HOMEOSTATIC/INTEROCEPTIVE FEELING — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Scientifically important and directly blocks the claim that all feeling must follow a full emotion episode.
F6 SENSORY FEELING/SENSATION — PASS-SUBTYPE / BOUNDARY CATEGORY.
Shows that ordinary "feeling" reaches beyond emotion; exact boundary with sensation depends on terminology.
F7 CORE-AFFECT FEELING — PASS-SUBTYPE/THEORY FAMILY.
Valence-arousal feeling is a coherent affective construct but does not exhaust all feelings.
F8 METACOGNITIVE/EPISTEMIC FEELING — PASS-SUBTYPE.
Feeling of knowing, familiarity, confidence, difficulty, etc. are established metacognitive-feeling phenomena and are not reducible to emotional feeling.
F9 NOETIC/FELT-SIGNIFICANCE EXPERIENCE — PASS AS PROVISIONAL SUBTYPE/RESEARCH TARGET.
The older project established that broad noetic/feeling labels have prior-art overlap. Retain only as a candidate subtype, not a universal feeling mechanism.
F10 VALENCE/HEDONIC TONE — PASS-SUBTYPE/PROPERTY; FAIL AS WHOLE.
Pleasantness/unpleasantness is central to many feelings but neutral or differently structured phenomenal states prevent equating feeling with valence.
F11 ACCESS/INTROSPECTIVE STATE — BLOCKED/NEIGHBOR.
Phenomenal-access equivalence is contested. Do not define feeling as access without choosing a consciousness theory.
F12 SELF-REPORT — FAIL AS DEFINITION; PASS AS MEASUREMENT CHANNEL.
Report is influenced by access, language, memory, confidence and demand. It cannot define the phenomenal state.
F13 PHENOMENAL MODE/PROPERTY — PASS-CORE, HIGH-LEVEL.
A powerful cross-domain interpretation: "feeling" may denote the phenomenal mode in which heterogeneous contents become present rather than one content-generating mechanism.
F14 HETEROGENEOUS FAMILY TERM — PASS.
Strongly supported by bodily, emotional, sensory and metacognitive uses. No single generator is currently established.
F-RESULT
The strongest surviving structure is F13 + F14: feeling is a heterogeneous phenomenal family/mode, with F3/F4/F5/F6/F7/F8 as content/generator subfamilies. F12 remains only a measurement variable. This generates F15.

F. GENERATION-2 CANDIDATES CREATED BY COMPARISON
==================================================
M10 — CONSTITUTIVE-VIABILITY SELF-MAINTENANCE
Combine viability regulation with organizational regeneration:
a living system maintains both viability-relevant state trajectories and the organization/constraints that enable its own continued regulation.
I16 — DEVELOPMENTALLY CANALIZED PREPARED POLICY LANDSCAPE
Combine canalization, policy priors and developmental-attractor ideas:
a developmentally produced landscape of policy biases that is reproducible across a normal ecological range and precedes the task-specific learning used to acquire ordinary habits.
E16 — DUAL-LEVEL EMOTION MODEL
E_func: a third-person functional coordination pattern across appraisal/relevance, physiology, action readiness, attention/valuation, expression and learning/decision systems.
E_episode: the full theory-dependent emotion episode; some theories include conscious feeling as one component, while E_func deliberately does not.
F15 — PHENOMENAL FAMILY/MODE MODEL
Feeling is the phenomenal mode/family in which bodily, emotional, sensory, metacognitive and other contents can be experienced. Different feeling families may have different generators. Report/access are observation processes, not the defining property.
N25 — MOTIVATIONAL CONTROL STATE
Generated because drive/motivation repeatedly appeared as neither instinctive action disposition nor emotion itself. N25 changes action direction/vigor/persistence based on need, incentive and expected outcomes.
N26 — AFFECTIVE BACKGROUND STATE
Generated from core affect + mood + arousal/valence comparisons. A slower/background affective condition may modulate emotion episodes without being identical to an emotion episode.
