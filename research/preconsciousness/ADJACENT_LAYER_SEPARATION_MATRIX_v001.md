ADJACENT-LAYER SEPARATION MATRIX v001
Date: 2026-10-07
PURPOSE
Test whether the candidate constructs are actually different rather than merely differently named.
M = self-maintenance / viability regulation
D = motivational/drive state
A = canalized/innate action-control architecture
E = emotion-state
Fh = homeostatic/interoceptive feeling
Fe = emotional feeling
PAIR 1 — M vs D
Status: SEPARABLE.
M can operate automatically without felt or motivational urge; physiological regulation includes many unconscious loops.
D changes behavioral vigor/selection based on need, anticipated outcome, and incentive.
Prediction: interventions can alter motivation without proportionally altering the regulated variable itself, and can alter physiological regulation without generating equivalent motivation.
PAIR 2 — M vs A
Status: SEPARABLE.
Single cells and bacteria exhibit homeostatic regulation without nervous-system motor policy architecture.
Action-control is a sensorimotor/policy-selection problem, not merely regulation of internal variables.
PAIR 3 — D vs A
Status: SEPARABLE BUT COUPLED.
Drive/motivation modulates probability, vigor, persistence, and priority of actions.
A is the policy/motor architecture that produces or selects behavior.
Same policy can be weakly or strongly expressed under different drive states.
Action-selection reviews explicitly treat motivation/drive as internal states regulating innate behaviors.
PAIR 4 — A vs E
Status: SEPARABLE BUT COUPLED.
Innate/motor patterns can be generated by specialized circuits such as central pattern generators.
Emotion states are persistent, scalable, valenced, generalizable, coordinating internal states that influence multiple behaviors and cognitive systems.
Therefore a motor policy is not itself an emotion state.
PAIR 5 — E vs Fe
Status: SEPARABLE UNDER FUNCTIONALIST DEFINITIONS.
Emotion-state can be operationalized through coordinated functional changes without making conscious experience part of its definition.
Threat/defensive processing and conditioned physiological responses can occur without conscious awareness in some paradigms.
Fe is the conscious experience associated with an emotion-state.
Caution: some theories define emotion partly by feeling, so terminology is theory-dependent.
PAIR 6 — M/interoception vs Fh
Status: DIRECT RELATION SUPPORTED; DOES NOT REQUIRE FULL E.
Hunger, thirst, pain, dyspnea and temperature discomfort are homeostatic/interoceptive feelings.
Most interoceptive regulation is unconscious; a subset becomes conscious feeling.
This breaks the universal rule E -> F if F is defined broadly.
PAIR 7 — D vs feeling/pleasure
Status: SEPARABLE.
Reward neuroscience dissociates motivational "wanting" from hedonic "liking".
Dopamine-related incentive salience can increase wanting without increasing pleasure.
This is a strong reason not to collapse drive/motivation into feeling or emotion.
PAIR 8 — A vs learned habit
Status: SEPARABLE IN DEGREE, NOT BY SIMPLE BIRTH/GENE RULE.
Species-typical action biases can depend on normal developmental experience.
Therefore A must be defined by developmental canalization/reliability and dependence on task-specific learning, not by "hardwired" or "genetically determined."
ARCHITECTURE RESULT
The strict sequence M -> instinct -> E -> F is not a universal dependency chain.
Best current graph:
M -> D
M -> interoceptive signals
D + sensory/context/history -> A
D + sensory/context/appraisal/history -> E
interoceptive signals + conscious-capable system -> Fh
E + conscious-capable system -> Fe
E -> A
F/cognition/learning -> recurrent feedback to D/A/E and indirectly M
DECISION
The original four-category framework survives only as a coarse research scaffold.
Mechanistic science currently requires at least M, D, A, E, and F, with F subdivided into Fh and Fe.
