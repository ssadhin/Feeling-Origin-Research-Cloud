EVIDENCE ANCHORS USED IN THIS ROUND
- NIMH RDoC currently separates arousal, motivation/valence-related systems, motor action, habit, and innate motor patterns rather than collapsing them into one "instinct" or emotion construct.
- Human Affectome separates/relates sensation, feeling, emotion, mood, wellbeing, valence, arousal, motivation, stress, self, etc., and treats subjective report as a measurement rather than feeling itself.
- Russell/Barrett core-affect work distinguishes core affect from prototypical emotional episodes.
- Adolphs-style functional approaches distinguish emotion states from conscious emotion experiences.
- Component Process Model / componential emotion work treats emotions as dynamically synchronized multicomponent episodes.
- Damasio/Carvalho treats feelings as mental experiences of body states, including homeostatic and emotional examples.
- Metacognitive-feeling literature establishes familiarity, confidence, feeling-of-knowing, difficulty, etc. as a distinct feeling family.
- Formal Emotion Ontology distinguishes emotion processes, appraisals, action tendencies, subjective feelings, and related entities.
- Biological-autonomy work distinguishes constitutive organization from regulatory responses that preserve viable organization under perturbation.
STATUS KEY
PASS = survives as a distinct scientifically useful candidate.
PARTIAL = captures a real component/subtype/formalism but is not sufficient as the general definition.
FAIL = unsuitable as the target definition.
BLOCKED = possible but too theory-loaded or insufficiently identifiable to serve as the definition.
NEIGHBOR = scientifically real but should not be identified with the target word.

M — SELF-MAINTENANCE OPTIONS
==================================================
M1 FIXED-SETPOINT HOMEOSTASIS — PARTIAL.
Explains defended variables and negative feedback.
Fails because biological regulation can use context-dependent reference ranges and predictive/allostatic shifts.
Generated lesson: fixed setpoint is a mechanism family, not the definition of all self-maintenance.
M2 DYNAMIC HOMEOSTASIS — PARTIAL.
Improves M1 by allowing changing defended ranges.
Still focuses on regulated physiological variables and need not capture repair/self-production/organizational maintenance.
M3 ALLOSTATIC/PREDICTIVE REGULATION — PARTIAL.
Captures anticipatory adjustment to demand.
Too narrow as a complete definition because self-maintenance also includes reactive regulation, repair and constitutive turnover.
M4 VIABILITY-REGION / VIABILITY-KERNEL CONTROL — PASS AS FORMAL ABSTRACTION.
Mechanism-neutral and can represent multiple regulatory strategies.
Weakness: viability set must be independently specified; otherwise the model becomes circular or arbitrarily broad.
M5 ORGANIZATIONAL CLOSURE / AUTOPOIETIC SELF-PRODUCTION — PARTIAL/PASS AS A DISTINCT DEEPER SUBCONSTRUCT.
Captures the fact that living systems maintain the organization that produces their own components/constraints.
Weakness: broader and more difficult to operationalize than physiological homeostasis; not identical to online regulation.
Important generation result: constitutive self-production and regulatory control should be separated rather than silently bundled.
M6 METABOLIC/REPAIR MAINTENANCE — PARTIAL.
Clear real maintenance mechanisms.
Not a complete definition because it omits many regulatory control processes.
M7 ACTIVE-INFERENCE / SELF-EVIDENCING — BLOCKED AS DEFINITION; PASS AS OPTIONAL FORMALISM.
Can model organismic regulation/prediction/action in one mathematics.
Fails as a definition because accepting active inference would commit the ontology to one contested formal theory.
M8 PERSISTENCE/SURVIVAL-PROBABILITY OBJECTIVE — FAIL AS DEFINITION; PARTIAL AS OUTCOME METRIC.
Continued existence may result from external protection/passivity without endogenous self-maintenance.
Survival probability does not identify the regulatory mechanism.
M9 MULTISCALE VIABILITY REGULATION — PASS WITH REFINEMENT.
Strongest Generation-1 umbrella, but comparison with M5 reveals a hidden conflation:
(a) constitutive self-production/repair and
(b) regulation that counters perturbation.
M-GENERATED CANDIDATE
G2-M:
Self-maintenance is a FAMILY with at least two coupled levels:
M_C = constitutive maintenance/self-production;
M_R = regulatory viability control.
V = viability constraint/criterion is separate from the mechanisms M_C and M_R.

I — INSTINCT / PREPARED-ACTION OPTIONS
==================================================
I1 GENETICALLY HARDWIRED PROGRAM — FAIL.
Genetic influence does not imply a complete pre-specified behavioral program; development and environmental input matter.
I2 PRESENT-AT-BIRTH — FAIL.
Timing of first expression does not define the causal/developmental architecture and excludes later-maturing species-typical behaviors.
I3 EXPERIENCE-INDEPENDENT — FAIL.
Species-typical behaviors can require species-typical developmental experience.
I4 SPECIES-TYPICAL — PARTIAL.
Population regularity is evidence for a prepared disposition but is not sufficient; learned/cultural behaviors can also be species-typical.
I5 FIXED/MODAL ACTION PATTERN — PARTIAL.
Legitimate ethological subtype, but behavior is often less fixed than classical terminology suggests.
I6 REFLEX — PASS AS DISTINCT SUBTYPE, FAIL AS SYNONYM FOR INSTINCT.
Reflexes are relatively direct sensorimotor transformations and do not exhaust prepared behavioral organization.
I7 RDoC INNATE MOTOR PATTERN — PASS AS NARROW OPERATIONAL CONSTRUCT.
Officially distinguished from habit and from broader affective/motivational domains.
Weakness: "unlearned" can still require careful developmental interpretation.
I8 ICAD — PASS.
Robustly separates task-specific acquired habit from developmentally prepared action bias.
Weakness: one ICAD definition does not automatically include reflexes, central pattern generators, motivational drive, or every behavior historically called instinct.
I9 POLICY PRIOR / PREPAREDNESS — PASS AS FORMAL REPRESENTATION.
Clean computational translation of a pre-learning action bias.
Weakness: a prior can be learned developmentally; the mathematical form alone does not establish "innateness."
I10 DEVELOPMENTAL ATTRACTOR — PASS/PARTIAL.
Captures reliable developmental emergence without genetic preformation.
Weakness: needs explicit developmental state space and is harder to estimate experimentally.
I11 DRIVE/MOTIVATION — NEIGHBOR, NOT INSTINCT.
Changes vigor, persistence and choice; RDoC and reward literature distinguish motivation from motor patterns/arousal.
I12 ACTION TENDENCY — NEIGHBOR/PARTIAL.
Can be part of emotion or motivation and can be learned; not diagnostic of instinct.
I13 LEARNED HABIT — FAIL AS INSTINCT; IMPORTANT COMPARATOR.
RDoC defines habit as learned stimulus-response mappings and separately lists innate motor patterns.
I14 CENTRAL PATTERN GENERATOR / MOTOR PROGRAM — PARTIAL IMPLEMENTATION.
Explains generation of patterned movement but not developmental preparedness, policy choice or motivational gating.
I15 ACTION-SELECTION ARCHITECTURE — TOO BROAD.
Includes innate and learned policy selection; cannot define instinct without a developmental/preparedness qualifier.
I-GENERATED CANDIDATE
G2-I:
The ordinary word "instinct" is a family label, not one construct.
For mechanistic work split:
I_R = prepared reflex pathways;
I_P = prepared/canalized policy dispositions;
I_G = motor-pattern generators.
Keep D = drive/motivation as a separate neighboring state that gates these systems.
If one operational target is required, I_P is the cleanest successor to Generation-0 ICAD.

E — EMOTION OPTIONS
==================================================
E1 DISCRETE BASIC-EMOTION PROGRAM — PARTIAL.
Captures evolutionarily prepared patterns and has substantial empirical tradition.
Fails as a universal definition because discrete-pattern/natural-kind claims remain contested.
E2 PRIMARY-PROCESS AFFECTIVE SYSTEM — PARTIAL/THEORY-SPECIFIC.
Important cross-species affective-neuroscience option.
Terminology often makes affective feeling central, making it incompatible with a theory-neutral E-versus-F split.
E3 FUNCTIONAL CENTRAL EMOTION-STATE — PASS WITH A MAJOR CAVEAT.
Persistence, scalability, generalization, valence and causal coordination provide strong operational criteria.
Caveat: "central latent state" may be stronger than the evidence requires; a distributed interacting process can generate the same functional signatures.
E4 COMPONENT-PROCESS/SYNCHRONIZATION EPISODE — PASS.
Strongly supported as an integrative representation across appraisal, physiology, motivation/action tendency, expression and feeling.
Caveat: some versions include feeling as an emotion component, so E must be defined carefully when testing E-versus-F.
E5 APPRAISAL — PARTIAL/COMPONENT.
Appraisal can help elicit and differentiate emotion but is neither necessary in the same explicit form across all theories nor sufficient to constitute the whole episode.
E6 CORE AFFECT — PASS AS DISTINCT COMPONENT, FAIL AS FULL EMOTION.
Russell/Barrett explicitly distinguish core affect from prototypical emotional episodes.
E7 PSYCHOLOGICALLY CONSTRUCTED EMOTION — PASS AS A SERIOUS RIVAL ONTOLOGY.
Explains emotion instances from more basic ingredients and conceptualization.
Directly challenges E as one fixed natural-kind central state.
E8 ACTION-TENDENCY/MOTIVATIONAL STATE — PARTIAL.
Captures an important emotion component but cannot explain the whole multicomponent episode.
E9 INTEROCEPTIVE/BODY-STATE REPRESENTATION — PARTIAL.
Important contributor to affect/emotion and feeling; insufficient as the sole definition of all emotion.
E10 PREDICTIVE/ALLOSTATIC EMOTION — PARTIAL/THEORY-LOADED.
Powerful integrative mechanism; not established as the uniquely correct definition.
E11 DISTRIBUTED DYNAMICAL COORDINATION PATTERN — PASS.
Fits component synchronization and network evidence without forcing one anatomical/latent "emotion center."
Can implement persistent/scalable/generalizable episode-level organization.
E12 SOCIAL-RELATIONAL EPISODE — PARTIAL/SUBTYPE.
Important for social emotions but not exhaustive.
E13 EMOTION CONCEPT/CATEGORY/LABEL — DISTINCT NEIGHBOR TO EMOTION EPISODE.
Adolphs-style distinctions and constructionist work show that concepts/labels must not be silently equated with the functional episode.
E14 EXPRESSION/RESPONSE PATTERN — FAIL AS COMPLETE DEFINITION.
Expression/autonomic response is only one component and can dissociate from subjective or appraisal components.
E15 UMBRELLA/FAMILY TERM — PASS AS A TERMINOLOGICAL VERDICT.
The field uses "emotion" for different targets; formal work must name which target is intended.
E-GENERATED CANDIDATE
G2-E:
Replace "central latent emotion-state" with a less assumptive EMOTION COORDINATION EPISODE (ECE):
a temporally bounded dynamical organization/trajectory of interacting components that shows organism-relevant coordination, persistence, scalability and generalization.
A central latent state becomes one implementation hypothesis, not part of the definition.

F — FEELING OPTIONS
==================================================
F1 ALL PHENOMENAL CONSCIOUSNESS — FAIL AS THE GENERAL SCIENTIFIC DEFINITION OF "FEELING."
Some frameworks use "felt" for the qualitative aspect of all experience, but equating feeling with all phenomenality collapses vision, audition, thought, etc. and loses affective distinctions.
F2 AFFECTIVE PHENOMENAL EXPERIENCE — PASS AS A BROAD FAMILY DEFINITION.
Matches major affective-science usage better than F1.
Still too broad to imply a single generator.
F3 MENTAL EXPERIENCE OF BODY STATE — PASS AS A MAJOR SUBTYPE/ACCOUNT.
Damasio/Carvalho supplies strong prior art.
Too narrow for metacognitive feelings unless all are ultimately reduced to body-state experience, which is not established.
F4 SUBJECTIVE EMOTIONAL EXPERIENCE — PASS AS SUBTYPE.
Cannot define all feelings because homeostatic and metacognitive feelings exist.
F5 HOMEOSTATIC/INTEROCEPTIVE FEELING — PASS AS DISTINCT SUBTYPE.
Provides a decisive counterexample to any definition "feeling = conscious emotion only."
F6 SENSORY FEELING/SENSATION — PASS/PARTIAL.
Some affective taxonomies treat sensation as an affective experience; other usage separates sensory phenomenology from feeling.
F7 CORE-AFFECT FEELING — PASS AS COMPONENT/SUBTYPE.
Valence/arousal is widely used but not all feelings reduce cleanly to that two-dimensional space.
F8 METACOGNITIVE/EPISTEMIC FEELING — PASS AS DISTINCT SUBTYPE.
Examples include familiarity, confidence, feeling-of-knowing, difficulty and tip-of-the-tongue.
This is major evidence against "all feeling = body state" and "all feeling = emotion."
F9 NOETIC/FELT-SIGNIFICANCE — PARTIAL SUBTYPE.
Project's earlier feeling research supports keeping it separate from familiarity, fluency, coherence and explicit judgment until residual uniqueness is demonstrated.
F10 VALENCE/HEDONIC TONE — PARTIAL.
Important feature of many feelings; insufficient as a definition of all felt qualities.
F11 ACCESS/INTROSPECTION — FAIL AS IDENTITY.
Phenomenal and access/report distinctions show that access is a relation to experience, not necessarily the experience itself.
F12 SELF-REPORT — FAIL AS IDENTITY; PASS AS MEASUREMENT.
Human Affectome and metacognition literature explicitly treat subjective report as a measurement channel.
F13 PHENOMENAL MODE/PROPERTY — PASS AS A GENERATION-2 FORMAL HYPOTHESIS.
Explains why heterogeneous content families can all be "felt" without sharing one upstream generator.
Must not be presented as an established mechanism of consciousness.
F14 HETEROGENEOUS FAMILY TERM — PASS.
Best current lexical/ontological verdict: "feeling" covers multiple experiential families.
F-GENERATED CANDIDATE
G2-F:
Feeling should be represented as a CONTENT-INDEXED PHENOMENAL FAMILY rather than one causal node:
F_t^(c), where c identifies content/source family (homeostatic, sensory, emotional, metacognitive, etc.).
A possible higher-level formal operator Phi(.) marks phenomenal presentation, but the mechanism of Phi remains the consciousness problem and is not solved by this construct analysis.

NEIGHBOR-CONSTRUCT RESULT
==================================================
The option universe shows several variables that cannot be safely absorbed into M/I/E/F without losing scientifically recognized distinctions:
drive/motivation;
arousal;
valence/core affect;
mood;
sensation/perception;
interoception;
reward/valuation;
appraisal;
habit;
learning/memory;
attention/salience;
agency/volition;
metacognition/access.
RDoC explicitly distinguishes arousal from motivation and valence, separates innate motor patterns from habit, and separates sensorimotor action planning from other domains.
Human Affectome likewise maps sensation, emotion, mood, feeling, valence, arousal, motivation, stress and wellbeing as related but nonidentical terms.
