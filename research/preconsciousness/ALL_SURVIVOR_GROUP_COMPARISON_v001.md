==================================================
SINGLETONS
==================================================
{M}
Explains biological/physiological and constitutive self-maintenance.
Cannot explain prepared behavioral biases, emotion organization, or phenomenality.
Verdict: necessary domain for life regulation, insufficient mind/experience model.
{I}
Explains prepared/developmentally canalized biases in perception/learning/valuation/action.
Cannot define viability regulation, full emotion episodes, or feeling.
Verdict: meaningful preparedness family, insufficient organismic-affective model.
{E}
Explains temporally structured multi-component affective coordination.
Cannot by itself explain biological self-maintenance, developmental preparedness, or phenomenality in theories where feeling is separated.
Verdict: meaningful affective episode construct, insufficient complete architecture.
{F}
Targets first-person phenomenal/felt presence.
Cannot specify the upstream regulation/action/emotion mechanisms that generate content.
Verdict: necessary experiential target, insufficient functional mechanism.
==================================================
PAIRS
==================================================
{M,I}
Explains physiological regulation plus prepared control biases.
Can model adaptive behavioral contributions to self-maintenance.
Fails to distinguish full emotion organization or phenomenal feeling.
Relation: coupled but non-identical.
{M,E}
Explains body regulation plus organism-relevant affective coordination.
Strong in allostatic/interoceptive emotion theories.
Fails to capture developmental preparedness and broad phenomenal taxonomy.
Relation: M constrains/feeds E; E feeds back onto M.
{M,F}
Explains homeostatic/interoceptive feeling route.
Fails for broad emotion dynamics and prepared behavior.
Strong counterexample to a universal E-before-F chain.
{I,E}
Explains prepared affective modules, innate defensive/appetitive biases and emotion-action interaction.
Fails to capture constitutive self-maintenance and phenomenality.
Cannot be universally collapsed because learned/contextual emotions exist.
{I,F}
Explains prepared biases plus consciously felt urges/desires in some cases.
Fails because preparedness can operate unconsciously and feeling has many non-instinctive generators.
Requires motivation/desire bridge variables.
{E,F}
Captures emotional experience.
Strong for full componential emotion episodes.
Fails as exhaustive feeling taxonomy because homeostatic, sensory and metacognitive feelings can occur outside full E.
Fails as complete biological model because M/I remain distinct.

TRIPLES
==================================================
{M,I,E}
Explains biological regulation, preparedness and functional emotion.
This is a strong pre-phenomenal organismic control model.
It cannot answer whether/when states are consciously felt.
Verdict: functional/behavioral architecture without F.
{M,I,F}
Explains self-maintenance, preparedness, homeostatic/urge feelings.
Cannot explain the distinctive multi-component coordination of emotion episodes.
Verdict: insufficient; E adds nonredundant organization.
{M,E,F}
Explains body regulation, emotion organization and feelings.
Many adult human affective episodes could be described without explicit I.
However, it loses prepared learning/action biases and developmental constraints that matter for species-typical responses.
Verdict: viable local model for some phenomena, not full target architecture.
{I,E,F}
Explains preparedness, emotion organization and feeling.
Can describe many psychological/behavioral events while abstracting away from biological maintenance.
But cannot explain the organismic viability substrate or homeostatic regulatory processes.
Verdict: psychologically rich, biologically incomplete.

ALL FOUR
==================================================
{M,I,E,F}
Advantages:
preserves four non-equivalent target roles;
captures biological regulation, preparedness, emotion organization and phenomenal feeling;
permits direct M/interoception -> F_h and E -> F_e routes;
supports cross-level causal tests.
Failure as exhaustive ontology:
still requires neighboring variables such as motivation, arousal, core affect/mood, perception/interoception, learning/memory, appraisal/cognition, agency/volition, access/report and conscious desire/urge.
Verdict:
USEFUL TYPED SCAFFOLD, NOT EXHAUSTIVE TAXONOMY.
