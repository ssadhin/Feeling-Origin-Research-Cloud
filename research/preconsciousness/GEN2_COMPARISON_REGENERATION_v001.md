1. SELF-MAINTENANCE SURVIVOR COMPARISON
==================================================
Survivors:
M4 viability-region control;
M5 organizational closure/self-production;
M9 multiscale viability regulation.
KEY COMPARISON
M4 is primarily a FORMAL CRITERION/CONTROL ABSTRACTION.
M5 is primarily a CONSTITUTIVE ORGANIZATION account.
M9 is primarily a REGULATORY UMBRELLA.
They are not mutually exclusive competitors.
Categorization:
V = viability criterion/region.
M_C = constitutive maintenance/self-production/repair of the organization that makes the system a living system.
M_R = regulatory maintenance that adjusts state/behavior to preserve viable operation under perturbation.
Relations:
M_C ENABLES M_R because regulation requires an organized system capable of regulation.
M_R SUPPORTS/PROTECTS M_C by countering perturbations threatening constitutive organization.
V CONSTRAINS both by defining what counts as viable continuation.
M_C and M_R are not reducible to V, because a viability set does not specify a mechanism.
GENERATION RESULT
The Generation-0 variable M was useful but under-resolved.
New preferred formal decomposition:
M := (M_C, M_R, V).

2. INSTINCT SURVIVOR COMPARISON
==================================================
Survivors:
I7 innate motor pattern;
I8 ICAD;
I9 policy prior/preparedness;
I10 developmental attractor/canalization;
plus I6 reflex and I14 motor generator as valid subtypes/implementations.
KEY COMPARISON
I8, I9 and I10 are three views of overlapping structure:
- I8 = behavioral/developmental operational criterion;
- I9 = computational representation;
- I10 = developmental-dynamical account.
They can be grouped into a PREPARED POLICY DISPOSITION class.
I6 reflex and I14 motor-pattern generator do not collapse into that class:
a reflex is a relatively direct sensorimotor mapping;
a generator can produce patterned output;
a policy disposition biases selection among possibilities.
D = drive/motivation remains independent:
the same prepared action architecture can be expressed differently under different need/motivation states.
GENERATION RESULT
The single I variable is too coarse.
Preferred decomposition:
I_R = prepared reflex mappings.
I_P = prepared/canalized policy dispositions.
I_G = prepared motor-pattern generators.
D = drive/motivation, separate.
"Instinct" remains only a historical/umbrella word.

3. EMOTION SURVIVOR COMPARISON
==================================================
Survivors:
E3 functional central state;
E4 component-process/synchronization;
E7 constructionist episode;
E11 distributed dynamical coordination;
E15 umbrella/family verdict.
KEY COMPARISON
E3 and E11 make similar behavioral predictions when a distributed network state can be summarized by a latent variable.
They differ mechanistically:
E3 posits or models a central/latent coordinating state;
E11 permits coordination to be an emergent property of interactions with no privileged center.
E4 supplies an explicit component structure:
appraisal/relevance;
autonomic/physiological change;
motivation/action tendency;
expression;
subjective feeling in versions that include it.
E7 says emotion categories can be constructed from more basic ingredients/context/concepts rather than corresponding to fixed natural-kind programs.
COMMON SURVIVOR
All four serious options allow temporally organized, context-sensitive patterns involving multiple components.
What is not shared is a commitment to:
one central state;
fixed basic categories;
one elicitation mechanism;
feeling as necessary.
GENERATION RESULT
Define the theory-neutral target as an EMOTION COORDINATION EPISODE (ECE):
a temporally bounded trajectory of coordinated change across a specified component vector in relation to organism-relevant conditions.
Then test whether ECEs are best explained by central-state, component-process, constructionist, primary-process, predictive, or other mechanisms.
FORMALIZATION
Let
q_t = [appraisal/relevance, valence/core affect, arousal, autonomic/endocrine state, action tendency, attention, expression, learning/decision bias, interoceptive representation, ...].
q_(t+1) = F_E(q_t, s_t, body_t, history_t, context_t) + eta_t.
An emotion episode is represented as a trajectory class:
E_j = {q_(t:t+T) : q_(t:t+T) belongs to coordinated class C_j}.
Coordination can be operationalized by time-resolved coupling/coherence and by out-of-sample prediction of multiple components.
A single latent e_t is now an optional reduced-order model, not part of the definition.

4. FEELING SURVIVOR COMPARISON
==================================================
Survivors:
F2 affective phenomenal experience;
F3 body-state feeling;
F4 emotional feeling;
F5 homeostatic feeling;
F8 metacognitive feeling;
F13 phenomenal mode/property;
F14 heterogeneous family.
KEY COMPARISON
F3, F4, F5 and F8 cannot all be reduced to one known generator without extra assumptions.
They do share an experiential/phenomenal target in their ordinary scientific use.
F11 access and F12 report fail as identities but remain measurement/access variables.
F1 "all phenomenality" is too broad to be a useful synonym for feeling, because many conscious perceptual/thought contents are not ordinarily classified as feelings even though they have phenomenal character.
GENERATION RESULT
Replace one F node with a CONTENT-INDEXED PHENOMENAL FAMILY:
F_t^(c) = phenomenal state/quality with content-family c,
where c may include:
homeostatic/interoceptive;
sensory-affective;
emotional;
mood/background;
metacognitive/epistemic;
agentive;
social/relational;
other validated families.
Measurement:
r_t^(c) ~ P(r | F_t^(c), Access_t, language_t, memory_t, demand_t).
OPTIONAL COMMON-MECHANISM HYPOTHESIS
There may exist a phenomenality-enabling mapping/operator Phi such that:
F_t^(c) = Phi(z_t^(c), kappa_t).
But Phi/kappa are placeholders for a future consciousness mechanism, not discoveries from this research.
If different feeling families require different phenomenal mechanisms, the common-Phi option fails.

5. CROSS-FAMILY TYPE COMPARISON
==================================================
The surviving constructs occupy different ontological roles:
V — constraint/criterion.
M_C — constitutive process.
M_R — regulatory/control process.
I_R/I_P/I_G — reflex/policy/motor dispositions or mechanisms.
D — motivational state.
Core affect/valence/arousal — dimensional states/features.
ECE — dynamical episode/trajectory class.
F^(c) — phenomenal experiential states/families.
Access/report — metacognitive/measurement relations.
This destroys any model that treats all terms as four scalar "levels" of the same variable.

7. GENERATION-3 ARCHITECTURE OPTIONS PRODUCED
==================================================
G3-A — TYPED LATTICE / MULTISYSTEM MODEL
No universal four-stage chain.
Different construct types interact recurrently:
M_C <-> M_R;
M_R/body state -> interoceptive representation;
D/valuation/arousal modulate action selection;
I_P/I_R/I_G constrain prepared action;
appraisal/context/history + body/valuation/action systems jointly form ECEs;
multiple source systems can generate content that is phenomenally presented as F^(c);
Access/report samples F^(c) imperfectly.
G3-B — COMPONENT-ONLY EMOTION MODEL
There is no independent E entity beyond the coordinated trajectory of components.
"Emotion" is the episode/classification of component dynamics.
This is the minimal E model.
G3-C — LATENT-STATE EMOTION MODEL
A latent e_t causally coordinates components and earns independent status if interventions on e_t alter several outputs beyond direct component coupling.
This preserves Generation-0 E as an empirical implementation hypothesis.
G3-D — CONSTRUCTIONIST / CORE-AFFECT-FIRST MODEL
Core affect + conceptualization/context/appraisal construct emotion categories; E is not a fixed natural kind.
G3-E — APPRAISAL/COMPONENT-PROCESS MODEL
Event relevance/appraisal organizes synchronized component changes and potentially subjective feeling.
G3-F — PRIMARY-PROCESS / EVOLUTIONARY AFFECT MODEL
Evolutionarily ancient affective systems organize behavior and affect; terminology may place feeling inside emotion/affect rather than downstream.
G3-G — HOMEOSTATIC-FEELING-FIRST MODEL
Self-maintenance/interoception directly yields homeostatic feelings in feeling-capable organisms; these can influence action, cognition and emotional episodes.
This explicitly reverses the assumption that all F follows E.
G3-H — PREDICTIVE/ACTIVE-INFERENCE UNIFIED MODEL
Prediction/control over interoceptive and exteroceptive variables jointly implements regulation, action, affect and learning.
Phenomenality remains an extra identification problem.
G3-I — ACTION/MOTIVATION-FIRST MODEL
Drive and action tendency are primary organizing variables; emotion categories arise around changing action readiness and context.
G3-J — PHENOMENAL-FAMILY MODEL
There is no one Feeling mechanism; homeostatic, emotional, metacognitive, sensory and agentive feelings are separate generator families sharing only the phenomenal-level description unless a common consciousness mechanism is later established.
