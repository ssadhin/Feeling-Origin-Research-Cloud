PURPOSE
Try to generate materially new options by reversing causal assumptions, adding latent causes, changing dimensional/categorical status, adding enactive/ecological and cultural theories, and using boundary cases.
1. REVERSED CAUSAL DIRECTIONS
Feelings can regulate later emotion/action; action can change body state; concepts and learning can alter appraisal and physiology. Mature systems are recurrent. This creates no new ontological type and strengthens the recurrent architecture.
2. REMOVE E
A component-only model can represent emotion as the coordinated trajectory of appraisal, physiology, motivation/action tendency, expression, attention/learning and feeling without an extra causal E entity. This remains the minimal rival to a latent-state implementation.
3. ADD LATENT COMMON CAUSES
Latent concern/relevance, threat/opportunity, goal state and context can jointly drive several components. These map to appraisal/relevance/context or to a theory-specific latent E implementation. Correlation among components does not prove a central emotion state.
4. DIMENSIONAL VS CATEGORICAL
Valence, arousal, dominance/control and continuous appraisal can span categories; basic-emotion, primary-process and discrete-action systems supply categorical alternatives. Neither representation replaces the other globally. Category/dimension is a modeling choice at a given level.
5. ENACTIVE / ECOLOGICAL / AFFORDANCE
Organism-environment actionability can be made explicit as a relational variable. Human Affectome and emotion-in-action traditions support this. Add environment/affordance relation R_env to context/action/appraisal mechanisms. It refines the lattice but creates no new top-level type.
6. SOCIAL / CULTURAL CONSTRUCTION
Cultural learning, social scripts and language alter emotional categorization, appraisal, expression and regulation. Add C_culture/history to ECE and learning/context. This is a developmental/contextual mechanism rather than a replacement for biological regulation or phenomenality.
7. MOOD / TIMESCALE
Mood is a prolonged or diffuse affective state and does not fit cleanly into a short event-linked ECE. Add mood as a neighboring long-timescale affective trajectory. This reinforces that the original four are non-exhaustive
8. AGENCY / OWNERSHIP FEELINGS
Agency literature distinguishes a low-level feeling of agency from explicit judgment of agency, and agency from ownership. Add F_agentive and F_ownership to the content-indexed feeling family. This strengthens the F-family model.
9. PAIN DISSOCIATION
Pain research separates sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational dimensions, and pain-asymbolia cases show that sensory pain-related experience and aversive significance/reactivity can dissociate. Therefore even one feeling family may require an internal multidimensional representation:
F_pain = (sensory-discriminative quality, unpleasantness/affective-motivational quality, secondary significance).
This strongly rejects one scalar feeling variable.
10. METACOGNITIVE FEELINGS
Familiarity, confidence, feeling-of-knowing, difficulty, certainty, coherence and tip-of-the-tongue concern cognitive processing rather than only body regulation or emotion episodes. Retain F_meta as an independent proximal-generator family.
11. DRIVE / ACTION TENDENCY VS FELT URGE
Motivational drive and action readiness are functional states; a conscious urge is their possible phenomenal counterpart. Do not collapse D/action tendency into F_urge.
12. HOMEOSTATIC-FEELING-FIRST
Theories in which homeostatic feelings are foundational to subjectivity strengthen a direct M/interoception -> F_homeostatic route. They do not imply that every feeling is an emotion.
13. ACTIVE-INFERENCE UNIFICATION
A common predictive-control mathematics can represent regulation, interoception, action and affect, but common formalism does not prove ontological identity. Phenomenality is not derived merely from free-energy minimization.
