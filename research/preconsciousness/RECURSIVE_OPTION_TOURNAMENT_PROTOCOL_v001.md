RECURSIVE OPTION TOURNAMENT PROTOCOL v001
Date: 2026-10-07

Authority: Explicit user methodology correction, consistent with the project's option-tournament and cumulative comparison rules.
PURPOSE
Do not stop at the first better model. The research must recursively generate, test, compare, categorize, recombine, and attack candidate explanations until the search converges under explicit stopping criteria.
CORE LOOP
ROUND 0 — DEFINE THE QUESTION AND REPRESENTATION
Freeze:
- target question;
- constructs;
- evidence types;
- mathematical representation;
- what counts as a distinct option;
- what counts as equivalent/redundant;
- falsification rules;
- stopping criteria.
ROUND 1 — GENERATE THE OPTION UNIVERSE
Generate every materially distinct option expressible within the current representation.
Where the space is finite:
- enumerate exhaustively by computation.
Where the scientific space is open-ended:
generate options systematically from:
1. established theory families;
2. variable inclusion/exclusion;
3. alternative causal directions;
4. serial, parallel, branching, recurrent and latent-common-cause structures;
5. continuous vs categorical formulations;
6. state vs process vs disposition vs trait vs measurement-model interpretations;
7. necessary/sufficient/neither/both relations;
8. developmental/evolutionary alternatives;
9. mechanistic vs phenomenological definitions;
10. rival mathematical formalisms;
11. negations and boundary cases;
12. hybrids/compositions of surviving options;
13. options generated from prior failures;
14. options generated from unexplained residuals;
15. options generated by removing shared assumptions from rival models.
Every option receives a unique ID and explicit testable form.
ROUND 2 — ONE-BY-ONE DESTRUCTION
Each option is evaluated independently before ranking.
For each option:
- strongest supporting evidence;
- strongest counterevidence;
- prior art;
- mathematical coherence;
- internal contradictions;
- identifiability;
- empirical observability;
- causal predictions;
- counterexamples;
- edge cases;
- simpler rivals;
- failure/kill condition;
- status: PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL / BLOCKED / EQUIVALENT.
Do not rescue an option by silently changing its definition. Any legitimate refinement becomes a NEW option.
ROUND 3 — ALL-PAIR / ALL-GROUP COMPARISON
Compare:
- every surviving option against every other survivor;
- each survivor against important failed rivals;
- every plausible group/combination;
- full all-survivor model;
- minimal subsets.
Record relations:
SUPPORTS
ENABLES
REQUIRES
REFINES
CONSTRAINS
CONTRADICTS
FAILED-RIVAL
INDEPENDENT
EQUIVALENT
UNRESOLVED
ROUND 4 — CATEGORIZE
Cluster candidates by:
- ontological type;
- causal architecture;
- mathematical equivalence;
- empirical equivalence;
- mechanism family;
- required assumptions;
- evidence profile;
- failure mode.
Options that are observationally/causally equivalent under available evidence are grouped rather than double-counted.
ROUND 5 — GENERATE NEW OPTIONS FROM COMPARISON
Use differences, commonalities, failures and unexplained residuals to generate NEW candidates.
Required generation operations:
A. merge complementary survivors;
B. split over-broad constructs;
C. remove unnecessary components;
D. add missing latent/common-cause variables;
E. reverse contested arrows;
F. add bypass paths exposed by counterexamples;
G. convert categorical variables to dimensions and vice versa;
H. create hybrid mechanisms;
I. create minimal versions of complex survivors;
J. create maximal integrated models;
K. generate models that explain the strongest failures of current leaders;
L. generate models from residuals left unexplained by all survivors.
New candidates return to ROUND 2.
Do not proceed to a final answer while genuinely new non-equivalent candidates are still being generated.
ROUND 6 — RECURSIVE WAR
Repeat:
GENERATE -> DESTROY -> COMPARE -> CATEGORIZE -> REGENERATE.
All failures remain in the ledger and can generate descendants.
ROUND 7 — CONVERGENCE TEST
The search may stop only when all of the following hold:
1. FINITE SUBSPACE EXHAUSTION
Every finite option space defined by the frozen representation has been exhaustively enumerated.
2. THEORY-FAMILY COVERAGE
All major serious existing theory families and obvious boundary/rival families have been represented or explicitly excluded with reason.
3. GENERATIVE SATURATION
At least two consecutive regeneration rounds produce no new materially non-equivalent candidate that survives initial hard checks.
4. COMPARISON SATURATION
Every surviving candidate and plausible survivor-group has been compared against all other survivors and the strongest failed rivals.
5. RESIDUAL TEST
No major empirical phenomenon/evidence class relevant to the question remains unexplained without being explicitly marked unresolved.
6. ADVERSARIAL SATURATION
Red-team attacks, counterexamples, assumption removal, reversal tests and simpler-rival searches fail to generate a new surviving option.
7. IDENTIFIABILITY BOUNDARY
Any remaining indistinguishable theories are formally shown to be empirically equivalent/underdetermined under current accessible evidence rather than arbitrarily selecting a winner.
8. LITERATURE SATURATION
Additional searches stop producing new theory families or decisive evidence that changes the option taxonomy.
9. FORMAL SATURATION
Mathematical representations expose no untested alternative parameterization/architecture that changes the substantive predictions.
10. PAPER GATE
Only after convergence is the final result evaluated for novelty, paper worthiness and exact permissible claims.
IMPORTANT EPISTEMIC RULE
For an unbounded scientific hypothesis space, "every possible option" cannot mean every conceivable sentence or future theory.
Operationally it means:
- exhaustive enumeration of every finite formal space we define;
- systematic generative coverage of the open scientific space;
- recursive generation until non-equivalent surviving options saturate;
- explicit proof of any remaining underdetermination.
