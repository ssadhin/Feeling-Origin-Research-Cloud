# Loop 005 — Strong Affective-Inertia Baseline Novelty / Publication Audit after O002-S1\n\n## Scope\n\nThis note audits a narrow candidate paper claim. It does not revive CRFI and does not infer phenomenal identity or causal body-to-feeling necessity.\n\nCandidate question: In prospective subject-independent forecasting of continuously self-reported valence/arousal, how much incremental predictive information does physiology provide after conditioning on stimulus/context and the person's immediately prior same-axis self-report?\n\nThe claim under audit is incremental value beyond affective inertia, not whether physiology correlates with affect or can predict affect above chance.\n\n## Frozen project evidence\n\nO001: 19 participants, prospective LOSO. Strong baseline = stimulus + immediately prior same-axis self-report. Primary Ridge result: no general physiology increment. O001-S1 nonlinear sensitivity had one small model-specific survivor: ExtraTrees valence delta +0.012409 MAE, 95% CI [0.004149, 0.020551]. O001-R1 failed its minimum-data gate with 7 eligible subjects. Therefore a universal zero-physiology claim is forbidden.\n\nO002 CASE external replication: 30 participants, 3300 prospective windows, 29 peripheral features. Primary NO_PERIPHERAL_INCREMENT for both axes. Valence baseline-minus-physiology delta -0.0037047, 95% CI [-0.0056178,-0.0018815]. Arousal delta -0.0047271, 95% CI [-0.0069045,-0.0024564].\n\nO002-S1 nonlinear sensitivity: cloud run 37218283683, permanent summary commit e9658296f0023e022b60517ee7a60d42dfaa62fd. Verdict STRONG_DEGRADATION. HGB valence -0.0124408 CI [-0.0162904,-0.0085447]; HGB arousal -0.0132776 CI [-0.0189772,-0.0077230]; ExtraTrees valence -0.0136297 CI [-0.0228868,-0.0035761]; ExtraTrees arousal -0.0226734 CI [-0.0306924,-0.0154384]. O002 no-increment is therefore not merely a linear-Ridge artifact.\n\n## Closest prior art\n\nColes et al. 2025, Royal Society Open Science, Big team science reveals promises and limitations of machine learning efforts to model physiological markers of affective experience. https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241778\n\nKey comparator facts: 12 teams predicted CASE affect reports from peripheral physiology; 100% of tests beat random baselines; only 46% beat a mean-rating baseline; selected top models usually degraded when real physiology was replaced with simulated Gaussian randomness. Critically, the paper explicitly notes that stronger baselines could preserve subject-specific means, first-order signal statistics, continuity, temporal variability and autocorrelation.\n\nNovelty consequence: it is not novel to show CASE physiology carries affect-related predictive signal, and not novel to show conclusions depend on validation regime. A potentially novel contribution is to operationalize the stronger temporal/autocorrelation benchmark the 2025 paper identifies, using immediately prior self-report as an affective-inertia baseline and strict prospective windows.\n\nDollack et al. 2023, Ensemble Learning to Assess Dynamics of Affective Experience Ratings and Physiological Change. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACIIW59127.2023.10388116 — predicts continuous CASE affect from physiology and uses stimulus identity in across-subject modeling, but does not establish incremental value beyond an immediately prior self-report baseline.\n\nD'Amelio et al. 2023, Affective Computing as a Tool for Understanding Emotion Dynamics from Physiology. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACIIW59127.2023.10388155 — shows past physiological features can outperform future features, but does not test the strong lagged-self-report baseline.\n\nMercado-Diaz et al. 2026, Continuous Emotion Recognition Using EDA-Graphs. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16073240 — strong LOSO CASE prediction from EDA-derived features, but not incremental value beyond recent reported affect plus context.\n\n## Strongest surviving paper thesis\n\nPhysiological signals can carry affect-related information, but their incremental subject-independent forecasting utility may be substantially reduced, fragile, or sometimes negative once a strong affective-inertia baseline (recent self-reported affect plus context) is modeled.\n\nThis reconciles EPiC evidence that real physiology contains signal with O001/O002 evidence that the signal may be redundant with recent affect state/context for short-horizon forecasting.\n\n## Novelty classification\n\nPOTENTIALLY PUBLISHABLE BUT NEEDS VALIDATION.\n\nReasons: directly operationalizes a temporal/autocorrelation baseline gap identified by the 2025 EPiC paper; prospective windows; held-out subjects; external replication; model-class sensitivity; transparent negative results.\n\nRemaining weaknesses: O001 nonlinear valence exception; O001-R1 failed data gate; O002 no-increment does not by itself distinguish absent signal from real-but-redundant signal.\n\n## O002-P1 frozen reconciliation test\n\nProtocol d3da8eefa9129b39f3a377deb1c38c1f69831c08; analysis eee6f2a7a2f410c757fce9abc307ae517fcead90;
